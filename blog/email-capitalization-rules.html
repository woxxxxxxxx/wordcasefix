Email Capitalization Rules for Professional Messages

By WordCaseFix | Updated July 29, 2026 | 7 min read
The practical default Use sentence case for a routine subject line, capitalize the first word and proper nouns in the message, capitalize names and formal titles attached to names, and capitalize only the first word of a multiword sign-off such as Best regards.

Professional email does not need special capitalization everywhere. The clearest approach is to treat most email text like ordinary prose and reserve capital letters for the beginning of a sentence, proper nouns, brands, and formal names.

Subject lines: sentence case or title case?

There is no universal rule that forces every email subject into one style. Sentence case is a useful modern default because it reads like a concise sentence. Title case can still work for formal announcements or an organization that consistently treats subject lines as headings.

Sentence case

Quarterly forecast ready for review

Title case

Quarterly Forecast Ready for Review

Harder to scan

QUARTERLY FORECAST READY FOR REVIEW

Too informal for many contexts

quarterly forecast ready for review

Choose one pattern for the team and use it consistently. A specific house style or client requirement takes priority over a general recommendation.

Greetings and names

Capitalize the first word of the greeting and every proper name. Generic descriptions normally remain lowercase.

UseExampleWhy
Named recipientHi Maya,The greeting begins with a capital and Maya is a proper name.
Formal recipientDear Dr. Chen:The title is attached to the person's name.
Team greetingGood morning, team,Only the first word and proper nouns need capitals.
Generic roleDear hiring manager,The role is descriptive rather than a person's formal name.

Sign-offs: capitalize the first word

A multiword closing normally uses sentence-style capitalization. Capitalize the first word, then lowercase the remaining common words.

RecommendedAvoid
Best regards,Best Regards,
Kind regards,Kind Regards,
Warm regards,Warm Regards,
Thank you,Thank You,
Sincerely yours,Sincerely Yours,

Job titles in the message and signature

Capitalize a formal title when it directly precedes a person's name. Lowercase a title used as a general description or placed after the name in running prose. Signature blocks often capitalize a formal role as a label.

Before a name

I spoke with Vice President Alicia Chen.

After a name

Alicia Chen, vice president of marketing, will join.

Signature label

Alicia Chen
Vice President, Marketing

Generic role

Do not write: I am meeting our Vice President later.

Are email addresses case sensitive?

The domain after the at sign is not case sensitive, so Example.com and example.com identify the same domain. The local part before the at sign can technically be case sensitive, although most major email services treat it as case insensitive in practice.

For a new address, lowercase is the clearest convention. When copying an address supplied by someone else, preserve it exactly instead of changing its capitalization.

Brands, acronyms, and departments

  • Keep the official capitalization of brands such as LinkedIn, YouTube, and iPhone.
  • Keep established acronyms uppercase: Q3, CPA, API, and PDF.
  • Lowercase a generic department reference: send this to marketing.
  • Follow the organization's own form when the department has an official name.
  • Do not add capitals to common nouns merely for emphasis.
Before sending
  • Use one capitalization style in the subject.
  • Check names, brands, product names, and acronyms.
  • Lowercase generic job titles and department names.
  • Use sentence-style capitalization in a multiword sign-off.
  • Read the final message once on a narrow mobile-sized window.

Frequently asked questions

Should email subject lines use title case or sentence case?

Both can work. Sentence case is a clear default for everyday business email. Use title case when your organization consistently treats subject lines like formal headings.

Is "Best regards" capitalized?

Capitalize only the first word: Best regards. The same pattern applies to Kind regards, Warm regards, and Thank you.

Should "Dear" be capitalized?

Yes. It is the first word of the greeting. Also capitalize the recipient's proper name and a formal title attached to that name.
